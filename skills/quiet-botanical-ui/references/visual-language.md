## Brand character

The product should feel:

- Quiet
- Soft
- Natural
- Clear
- Restrained
- Breathable
- Refined without luxury theater
- Professional without looking clinical
- Warm without looking childish
- Editorial rather than SaaS-dashboard-like

Clarity and comfort outrank visual novelty.

## Visual DNA

The source language is quiet botanical skincare editorial:

- Warm neutrals
- Low saturation
- Generous negative space
- Soft geometry
- Tactile materiality
- Botanical accents
- Restrained typography
- Subtle imperfection
- Editorial composition

Translate these qualities into a digital interface. Do not copy a photo shoot, printed page, or physical texture literally.

## Digital translation

- Use warm surfaces and moderated contrast instead of adding visible paper texture everywhere.
- Use spacing and typography to create editorial rhythm.
- Use soft geometry consistently; reserve organic forms for accent moments.
- Keep asymmetry subtle and intentional. Interaction targets and reading order remain predictable.
- Treat decorative elements as supporting atmosphere, never as required controls or information containers.

## Imagery and illustration

Preferred image language:

- Quiet botanical subjects
- Warm neutral backgrounds
- Low saturation
- Matte finish
- Tactile but restrained detail
- Soft light
- Editorial negative space
- Clean product still life

Suitable illustration includes botanical studies, abstract organic shapes, and minimal product drawings.

Avoid generic stock-photo staging, glossy commercial beauty imagery, cyber-tech imagery, neon gradients, 3D cartoon rendering, and anime styling.

## Material translation

### Linen

Translate linen into warm neutral backgrounds, low contrast, and rare subtle texture. Do not apply visible fabric texture to every screen.

### Paper

Translate paper into matte surfaces, restrained borders, and editorial composition. Do not make every card look like a paper sticker.

### Frosted glass

Use translucency only when it clarifies an overlay, modal, bottom sheet, or image overlay. Do not use glassmorphism as the app's primary surface system.

## Motion

Motion should be slow enough to feel calm, brief enough to keep the interface responsive, and always functional.

Prefer:

- Fade
- Gentle slide
- Subtle scale
- Crossfade

Avoid:

- Bounce
- Elastic overshoot
- Exaggerated spring
- Spin
- Flashy transitions

Use motion to explain state change, hierarchy, continuity, or feedback—not to decorate an otherwise static screen.
